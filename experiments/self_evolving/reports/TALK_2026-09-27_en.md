# Talk script: document-driven forecasting with a self-evolving three-agent system

(About 12–15 minutes. The 【】 line at the top of each section is the slide headline.)

---

## 1. Problem and setting (1 min)

【A strong forecaster + documents: can the system learn by itself *when* and *how* to correct it?】

Our starting point: foundation models like Toto already forecast well from the numeric history alone. But many tasks come with documents that carry extra information, such as "holiday next week", "the sensor was faulty", or "no more maintenance from now on". The question is whether the system can **evolve by itself** what to extract from the documents, how to adjust Toto, and when to leave it alone, instead of relying on hand-written rules.

The system has three agents:
- **Numerical** sees only the numeric history.
- **Retrieval** sees only the documents and produces evidence or corrections.
- **Decision** can only choose among existing candidates or fall back to Toto; it never generates a forecast of its own.

Generation 0 is exactly Toto, so evolution always starts from "change nothing".

Data:
- **Dr-CiK**: train 80 / dev 20 / test 99.
- **Time-MMD**: train 477 / dev 181 / test 182.

Metrics are sMAE and sRMSE, lower is better. Toto on the Dr-CiK test set: **sMAE 0.3814, sRMSE 0.5903**.

## 2. Evaluation protocol (1 min)

【Evolve on train only; open dev once as a gate; report test at the end】

- **Evolution and selection use only the training set.** The main estimate is nested cross-validation on train, stratified by domain, which matches how train and test are split.
- **Dev gate:** neither sMAE nor sRMSE may get worse; otherwise the method falls back to Toto.
- **Document role labels** (which documents are distractors) are never shown to the models. **Ground-truth evidence annotations** are used only on train, as an evolution signal.
- In full honesty: the test set has been opened several times since nrd3, so every later test number is **exploratory**.

## 3. How the versions evolved (4 min)

【Five stages: make all three agents evolvable → Numerical only judges → improve where corrections come from → stronger search → find the real use of the documents】

**Stage 1: from picking methods to evolving a dictionary**
- v1/v2: Numerical could only pick from 31 existing methods. Train-CV joint error stayed near Toto's 0.990. Starting from Toto, a single mutation could not take the first step.
- nrd3: Numerical **evolves its own dictionary**, and Decision blends dictionary methods into Toto. Train CV 0.990 → 0.940, but on test **38 tasks improve and 18 get worse**, and only 1 of 3 seeds passes dev.
- Diagnosis: switching the blending off raises train CV from +2.0% to +5.5% and cuts harmed tasks from 45 to 19. **Blending is the main source of harm.**

**Stage 2: Numerical no longer replaces Toto, it only supplies judgements (nrd4, main result)**
- Numerical outputs two history-only judgements: how much the series normally fluctuates, and how reliable Toto is for this kind of series. These become features for Retrieval's per-correction validator; Decision keeps only 2 parameters.
- Results:
  - train CV 0.990 → **0.927**;
  - dev passes on all 3 seeds (0.9792 → 0.9786);
  - test sMAE 0.3814 → **0.3681**, sRMSE 0.5903 → **0.5539**, with about **30 tasks improved and only 1–2 worse**.
- Ablation: without Numerical's judgements, train CV is only 0.964.

**Stage 3: trying to make the corrections themselves better (nrd5–nrd10)**
- We tried:
  - GPT-evolved extraction instructions;
  - a residual model with a conformal no-harm guarantee;
  - evidence verification plus a 3-model vote;
  - a learned window shift;
  - precise-time re-extraction;
  - merging two extractors.
- Common outcome: each gains something on train, but none beats nrd4 on test.
- Key diagnosis: only 29 of the 75 document corrections on train are helpful (39%). Of the 46 harmful ones:
  - 26 have the wrong time window;
  - 13 have the wrong direction;
  - 7 are too large.
- So **the problem is the extraction itself, not the filtering**.

**Stage 4: stronger search (nrd11, nrd12)**
- nrd11: evolve the dictionary first, then turn it into judgement features for the joint evolution. The dictionary features are used, and performance matches nrd4.
- nrd12: GEPA-style Pareto selection plus FunSearch-style islands. Best train CV (0.911, all 9 folds positive), but dev 1.0255 and 1.0286 fail the gate, and test is worse than nrd4.
- Lesson: with only 80 training tasks, **stronger search overfits**, and the dev gate is essential.

**Stage 5: finding the real use of the documents: history repair (tl, tl2)**
- Dr-CiK documents often say that "a past stretch of data was abnormal and will not happen again". Toto learns those anomalies as if they were normal patterns.
- Repair those stretches in the history and re-run Toto: test sMAE 0.3814 → **0.3369** (11.7% better), the best of all methods.
- It is not stable yet: dev 0.9792 → 1.0166, and on test 10 tasks improve while 13 get worse.

## 4. Deep dive 1: how tl2 works (2.5 min)

【Retrieval extracts a state timeline → Numerical repairs the history → Toto re-forecasts; the extraction instructions evolve against ground-truth evidence】

**Step 1: Retrieval extracts a timeline.** GPT reads all 70+ documents of a task and outputs intervals. Each interval has:
- a kind: measurement error, one-off disturbance, recurring pattern, state, or future event;
- start and end times, and whether it repeats daily or weekly (e.g. "every day 08:00–14:00");
- whether it will happen again in the future;
- the direction of its effect;
- **a verbatim quote**.

**Step 2: Numerical repairs the history.** For measurement errors, one-off disturbances and recurring anomalies that will not happen again:
1. the affected history steps are filled with the same-phase median;
2. Toto re-forecasts from the repaired history.

The magnitude comes entirely from the history itself: no LLM guessing and nothing fitted.

**Step 3: how the extraction instructions evolve.**
- **Fitness:** on a minibatch of 30 training tasks, the **F1** between the extraction and the ground-truth evidence annotations:
  - recall: how many of the actionable ground-truth items (those with timing or "will it recur" information) were extracted;
  - precision: how many extracted quotes are real evidence rather than distractor text.
- **Mutation operator:** GPT-6-sol. It receives the current instructions plus concrete train feedback (missed ground-truth sentences and wrongly extracted distractor quotes) and rewrites the instructions.
- **Selection:** 2 children per generation; a child is accepted only if it beats the current instructions. 4 generations.

**Results:**
- Evolution curve (F1): 0.054 → 0.206 → 0.276 → 0.292 → **0.464**.
- Full training set: recall 5.4% → **48.3%**, precision 9.1% → **67.0%**.
- Rules GPT discovered on its own, e.g.:
  - "apply another entity's event only when it is explicitly linked to the target";
  - "separate measurement errors from real, lasting changes";
  - "keep the exact scope of 'not again this week' versus 'never again'";
  - "never interpolate boundaries".
- Repair effect: train joint error 0.990 → 0.835 (15.7% better); test sMAE 0.3814 → 0.3369.

**What we fix next:** dev failures come from two things. Natural daily cycles are mistaken for anomalies (zero solar irradiance at night), and the fill-in method does not suit very noisy series. Plan: Decision accepts each repair only if a history-only back-test confirms it, and the fill-in method becomes something Numerical evolves.

## 5. Deep dive 2: how the dictionary self-evolves (2 min)

【Executable programs + typed mutations + history-only back-testing + MAP-Elites】

**Individuals:** each dictionary member is an executable forecasting program built from building blocks:
- **Base operators:** naive, seasonal naive, drift, SES, damped trend, Theta, moving average, rolling linear regression, seasonal profile, each with parameters (smoothing, window length, number of cycles…).
- **Combinators:** a weighted **blend** of two programs; a **router** that switches programs based on seasonal strength; **clipping** the forecast to near the historical range.
- A program has at most 5 nodes, with a size penalty so programs don't grow without limit.

**Typed mutations:** tweak parameters, swap in a new base operator, blend with another member, add a router, add or change clipping, or remove one layer of composition.

**Fitness uses only the history, never labels:** a rolling back-test inside each training task's history. We hold out the last H points and the H before them, forecast them, and **subtract Toto's back-test error at the same positions**. Fitness is therefore "how much better than Toto".

**MAP-Elites:** a grid of cells, frequency (second / minute / hour / day) × seasonal or not. Each cell keeps only its best program, and the elites across all cells form the dictionary. Each generation samples parents from the elites and produces children; a child replaces a cell's elite if it is better in that cell.

**Results:**
- The gap between the elites' and Toto's back-test joint error (averaged over cells) evolves from **+0.092 (worse than Toto)** at generation 0 to **−0.05 to −0.07 (better than Toto)**, ending with 6 elite programs.
- For example, in the "hourly × seasonal" cell, the elite's back-test joint error is about 0.29 lower than Toto's.

**How it is used** (lesson learned the hard way):
- Blending it directly into Toto made many tasks worse (nrd3).
- Now (nrd11) "how much the dictionary beats Toto in this cell or task" is a judgement feature for the joint evolution. Its weight is positive in all 9 folds: **the more the dictionary beats Toto, the more willing the system is to trust text corrections**.

## 6. Deep dive 3: how the three agents co-evolve (2 min)

【Each agent evolves → teams are formed → scored as a whole on train folds → accepted only if better, otherwise the round rolls back】

Each generation (15 in total):
1. **Numerical evolves:** the magnitude calibrator hill-climbs for 3 mini-generations with 12 children each. Its fitness is a history-only "how well it predicts the size of the next stretch's fluctuation". The per-cell Toto trust profile is updated too, and the two-stage version adds the dictionary features.
2. **Retrieval evolves:** the per-correction validator is a low-dimensional scorer. Its features are:
   - correction size;
   - confidence;
   - window length;
   - share of descriptive documents;
   - size relative to historical fluctuation;
   - Toto reliability.

   It outputs "accept / halve / reject" and is optimised with an evolution strategy (population 16, elite 4, 15 mini-generations) on **per-correction gain**, where harmful corrections are penalised 1.5×.
3. **Decision proposes variants:** only 2 parameters, correction strength and a trust gate, with 6 variants per generation.
4. **Joint selection:** 32 teams are sampled from the current and new versions of the three agents and scored as a whole on the stratified training folds:
   - fold score = mean gain + 0.5 × mean negative gain (a penalty specifically for tasks made worse);
   - team fitness = mean fold score − 0.25 × std across folds (rewards stability);
   - **a team is accepted only if it beats the current team; otherwise the whole round rolls back.**

**Why joint selection rather than each agent optimising alone:** the agents depend on each other. Retrieval's validator uses Numerical's trust feature, and a given validator only works well at certain Decision strengths. Scoring at the team level keeps the combination that works best together.

**The final learned rule (nrd4) is interpretable:**
- The validator's weight on "share of descriptive documents" is **strongly negative** (−2.4 to −3.7), on correction size positive (+1.5 to +3.9), and on Toto's unreliability positive (+0.3 to +1.0).
- In one sentence: **accept large corrections backed by event documents, reject corrections from descriptive or methodology documents, and trust documents more where Toto is less reliable.**
- On train, only 23 of the 75 corrections are accepted, and 18 of those (78%) help.

## 7. Conclusions (1 min)

【nrd4 is the stable main result; history repair has the highest ceiling; Time-MMD is the weak-text boundary】

1. Under the strict protocol, **nrd4 is the main result**: test sMAE 0.3814 → 0.3681, sRMSE 0.5903 → 0.5539, with almost no harm (about 30 improved, 1–2 worse).
2. **Numerical's right role is to judge, not to replace:** removing its judgements takes train CV from 0.927 back to 0.964.
3. **On small data, stronger search overfits:** nrd12 has the best train CV but worse dev and test, so the dev gate is essential.
4. **The most valuable use of documents is repairing the history:** tl2 reaches test sMAE 0.3369, the best of all methods. Next we make the "repair or not" decision stable, then combine it with nrd4's future corrections.
5. **Time-MMD:** the documents carry almost no forward-looking information, and every method stays near 0. The system correctly learns to leave Toto alone, an honest boundary result.

## 8. Likely questions

- **"The test set was opened many times; can we trust the results?"** The main result, nrd4, was selected by train CV + dev, not by test; the other test numbers are for analysis only. The cleanest final test would be Dr-CiK's other 80 tasks with hidden labels, which needs official evaluation.
- **"Is using gt_evidence leakage?"** It is used only on train as an evolution signal, the same way as train ground truth; it is never used on dev or test.
- **"How is this different from CompEvo and similar work?"**
  - We evolve executable, auditable programs: dictionary programs, validators and extraction instructions.
  - Decision can only choose among existing candidates or fall back to Toto.
  - Every round can roll back, there is a dev gate, and the objective explicitly penalises doing harm.
